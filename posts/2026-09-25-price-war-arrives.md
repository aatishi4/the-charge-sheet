Three things worth your time this week, and one thing I would do about them.

## 1. The price floor just moved

An InsideEVs comparison of 17 fast-charging networks put **Ionna at $0.37 per kWh**, the cheapest of the group, and **Walmart at $0.43**, fourth cheapest. Tesla and Electrify America came in at **$0.56**. Ionna has also run opening and holiday promotions in the twenty-cent range.

Walmart's own network averages about **$0.47 on-peak and $0.29 off-peak**, according to Tom Moloughney's September tracker, across 109 sites and 910 stalls in 21 states. Every stall is a 400 kW unit with both CCS1 and NACS.

**Why it matters:** these are two operators with structural advantages you probably don't have. Walmart already owns the parking lot, the transformer relationship and the foot traffic. Ionna is backed by eight automakers and is buying market share. Neither needs a site to pay back on its own schedule.

## 2. Build-out keeps outrunning demand

The US had **76,236 public DC fast-charging ports** at about 15,800 locations as of September 1, up 23% in a year, with 1,131 new stalls in August alone. Tesla still has roughly half of them. Ionna jumped to eighth place after adding 268 stalls in two months.

Meanwhile, Paren's Q1 report had national DC fast **utilization at 15.6%, down from 16.2%** a year earlier. More ports, slightly fewer minutes of use per port.

**Why it matters:** falling utilization makes demand charges a bigger share of every kWh you sell. A quiet charger still pays for its peak.

## 3. Reliability is inching up, slowly

The same Paren report put the national reliability average at **93.5%**. Better than it was, and still a long way from what a gas station would accept. Every failed session is a driver who remembers your address.

## What I would do

If you are underwriting a site today, stop assuming you can charge what the big networks charged last year. Run your [site planner](/ev-charging-site-planner/) numbers at a price twenty cents lower and see whether the site survives. If it only works at yesterday's price, it is a bet on your competitors staying expensive.

Then look at the demand charge line. The [business guide](/ev-charging-business-model/#p-demand) explains why that is where quiet sites go to die.
